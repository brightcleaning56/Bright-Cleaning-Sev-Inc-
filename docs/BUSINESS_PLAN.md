# Bright Clean — National Cleaning Services Business Plan

Bright Cleaning Sev Inc. | The Bright Clean Group
Business Strategy & Expansion Plan — 2026–2030

This document is the business plan the quoting and proposal system follows. Prices are proposed initial customer prices, subject to cost validation. Financial figures are planning scenarios, not forecasts or guaranteed results.

## 1. Executive summary

Bright Cleaning Sev Inc., operating as Bright Clean, is a professional cleaning and facility maintenance company providing residential, multifamily, commercial and specialty cleaning throughout the United States. The model is built on recurring monthly subscriptions, commercial service agreements, apartment portfolio contracts and specialty cleaning.

The company runs as an integrated business management platform, not as a company that quotes and schedules every job by hand. The platform manages customer acquisition and sales, residential subscriptions, apartment cleaning and turnovers, office and commercial cleaning, construction final cleaning, recruiting and workforce management, payroll and scheduling, insurance and risk, contracts and recurring billing, quality inspections, and financial reporting and profitability.

Primary objective: build a nationally recognized cleaning and facility services company with predictable recurring revenue, profitable service delivery, and the ability to operate across multiple states.

## 3. Services

**Division A — Residential:** single-family home cleaning, apartment cleaning, kitchen, bathroom, bedroom and living room cleaning, dusting and surfaces, vacuuming and mopping, deep cleaning, move-in and move-out cleaning, recurring housekeeping, seasonal cleaning.

**Division B — Multifamily and apartments:** unit cleaning, turnovers, vacant and occupied unit cleaning, move-out and move-in preparation, hallway and common-area cleaning, leasing office, clubhouse, fitness center and model unit cleaning, property inspections, emergency cleaning. Pursued through apartment management companies, property owners, real estate operators and multifamily investment groups.

**Division C — Commercial and office:** office janitorial, restroom sanitation, breakroom, conference room and lobby cleaning, floor maintenance, carpet cleaning, trash removal, disinfection, day porter services, after-hours cleaning, recurring facility maintenance.

**Division D — Specialty and facility services:** post-construction final cleaning, final touch-up, interior and exterior window cleaning, power washing, parking lot cleanup and maintenance, valet trash services, floor stripping and waxing, carpet extraction, commercial property maintenance. Specialty services are priced separately from standard cleaning subscriptions.

## 4. Business model and revenue

| Revenue channel | Business model |
|---|---|
| Residential subscriptions | Monthly recurring billing |
| Apartment subscriptions | Per-unit monthly or recurring service |
| Multifamily contracts | Portfolio-wide agreements |
| Office cleaning | Monthly commercial contracts |
| Specialty cleaning | Individual project contracts |
| Additional services | Approved add-ons and change orders |

Recurring contracts come first, because they make revenue predictable and let management plan staffing, equipment and capacity.

**Residential subscription options:** Monthly (one scheduled visit per month), Biweekly (two visits per month), Weekly (four visits per month), Custom (additional visits under an agreed plan). The agreement states how many visits are included. For months with an extra calendar week, the contract distinguishes a fixed monthly visit allowance from additional paid visits.

**Hybrid contract model:** (1) fixed recurring maintenance, (2) reserved additional cleaning capacity, (3) separately approved specialty and additional work. Property managers can contract for a whole portfolio and request cleaning for individual units on different dates. Billing is consolidated across the portfolio.

## 6. Employees and workforce

Employees are the foundation of service delivery. Categories: residential cleaners, apartment technicians, commercial janitorial employees, team leaders, site supervisors, quality inspectors, operations managers, drivers, administrative staff, sales representatives and regional managers. Independent contractors are used only where legally appropriate and properly classified.

**Planning assumption:** starting cleaner wage $20 per hour. Added employee costs: employer payroll taxes, unemployment insurance, workers' compensation, benefits where applicable, paid leave, training, uniforms, equipment, travel and nonproductive time. Compensation policies are set by state and job classification.

**Workforce technology:** onboarding, employee profiles, background-check workflows where appropriate, employment eligibility verification, training records, time clock, GPS job-site verification with privacy safeguards, scheduling, payroll integrations, availability, attendance, performance reviews, safety reporting and document management.

## 7. Insurance and risk

Coverage categories: general liability; workers' compensation; employers' liability; commercial auto; commercial umbrella; janitorial bonds or employee dishonesty coverage where appropriate; equipment and inland marine; commercial property; cyber liability; employment practices liability.

The company keeps an insurance directory (carriers, brokers, policies, limits, premiums, renewal dates, claims contacts). The system monitors expiration dates and raises alerts. Insurance cost is included in job costing and pricing. Customer contracts requiring specific limits are checked before approval.

## 8. Financial and profitability

**Target contribution margin: 30%.**

| Input | Amount |
|---|---|
| Base cleaner wage | $20 per hour |
| Planning payroll burden | 20% |
| Loaded paid-hour cost | $24 |
| Productive utilization target | 80% |
| Productive labor-hour cost | $30 |

The pricing engine calculates wages, payroll burden, workers' compensation, insurance allocation, cleaning supplies, equipment, travel, operating overhead, customer acquisition cost where applicable, and contract-specific expenses.

**Price formula:** Selling price = total cost ÷ (1 − target margin). At 30%: selling price = total cost ÷ 0.70.

Contribution margin is tracked separately from net profit. Corporate expenses, taxes, financing and other costs are still deducted to reach net profit.

## 10. Sales and marketing

**Residential targets:** homeowners, renters, busy professionals, families, seniors, real estate customers, property investors. Channels: Google Business Profile, search optimization, Google advertising, social media, referrals, direct mail, local partnerships. Goal: recurring monthly customers rather than one-time jobs.

**Commercial targets:** apartment management companies, property management firms, office buildings, general contractors, construction companies, commercial real estate owners, schools, medical offices (subject to training and compliance), retail centers, industrial facilities. Channels: direct outreach, commercial proposals, property manager relationships, vendor registration, construction bidding, facility partnerships, national accounts.

**Sales process:** 1 lead acquisition → 2 customer qualification → 3 property assessment → 4 scope definition → 5 automated estimate → 6 profitability approval → 7 proposal submission → 8 contract execution → 9 scheduling → 10 service delivery → 11 quality inspection → 12 recurring billing.

## 11. Operations

The national office manages pricing, financial reporting, insurance administration, customer standards, technology, national accounts, marketing and quality standards. Regional operations manage hiring, supervision, scheduling, local customer relationships, supplies, inspections and service delivery.

Every assignment has a defined scope, estimated labor hours, an assigned team, customer expectations, completion records and quality requirements.

## 12. Growth phases

1. **Foundation:** standardize pricing; finalize service catalogs; establish insurance; build employee onboarding and payroll; establish recurring contracts; complete the platform.
2. **Regional strength:** grow residential subscriptions; establish apartment portfolio contracts; develop commercial office accounts; build teams and regional supervisors; improve route density.
3. **National expansion:** enter more states; build regional management; establish national commercial accounts; develop property management partnerships; expand construction cleaning; introduce national account management.
4. **Enterprise scale:** multiple regional operating centers; larger national contracts; expanded workforce; advanced automation; purchasing and equipment efficiency; long-term strategic partnerships.
