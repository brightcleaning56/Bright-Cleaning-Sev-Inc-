1. Executive summary

DentistNow Robotics will develop AutoClean Pro 2.0, an AI-assisted dental examination and robotic preventive-cleaning station. The machine performs intake, medical screening, oral photography, 3D scanning and assessment, then routes findings to a licensed dental professional. Approved polishing and plaque removal happen only after eligibility checks, consent and professional authorization. Subgingival calculus removal, drilling, extraction and restorative work are out of scope for the first generation.

The strategy is staged. Prove the core technology with a small first raise, then commit to a functional prototype, then to full commercial development. Each stage ends with a decision gate. Non-dilutive funding and strategic partners run in parallel with equity.

Recommendation: raise Stage A first. Do not promise a finished autonomous machine until the technology has been shown to work.

2. Product and intended use

Intended use (draft, for regulatory review): an automated dental examination-support and cleaning system for adult patients, used in a clinic under the supervision of a licensed dental professional, to acquire oral images and scans, record medical and symptom information, flag findings for professional review, and perform validated polishing and plaque removal after authorization.

  • Version 1: scanning, imaging, screening, guided cleaning and polishing within validated limits.
  • Version 2: additional cleaning capability, subject to clinical validation.
  • Version 3: advanced procedures, only after safety and regulatory requirements are met.

Operating modes: First-Visit Examination, Approved Cleaning, and Follow-Up and Monitoring.

6. Funding strategy, source by source

No single source is expected to fund the program. The plan combines equity, non-dilutive grants and partnerships, with each step gated.

6.1 Private investors (equity)

  • Who: founders, angels, dental-practice owners, medtech investors and family offices.
  • Vehicle: priced equity or SAFE, selected with securities counsel.
  • Pitch material: the investor deck (to be built), this plan and the engineering binder. Share the binder only with counsel-approved recipients.
  • Target for Stage A: $150K–$300K, in a round of about 10–20 investors if sized at the low end.

6.2 Iowa: America's Seed Fund — Iowa Program terms confirmed

The Iowa Economic Development Authority (IEDA) runs this program. It helps Iowa companies obtain and apply for federal SBIR/STTR funding: idea review, professional proposal assistance, and commitments to matching funds. Confirmed terms from the IEDA program page:

  • Total support: $75,000.
  • Phase I: $50,000 grant on top of a federal grant or contract.
  • Phase II: $25,000 grant on submission of a federal grant or contract.
  • Eligibility: Iowa-based company, legally formed and registered with the Iowa Secretary of State, fewer than 500 employees, innovative and industry-agnostic. Phase I covers feasibility studies to validated concepts; Phase II covers field testing, prototyping and scaling.
  • Review: applications are reviewed on an ongoing basis. Eligibility and the current application are confirmed by contacting IEDA directly. The page gives a named contact, which we keep in the internal checklist rather than here.

The IEDA page lists a broken link on the same site. Confirm the contact details before use.

Action for us: register DentistNow Robotics in Iowa with the Secretary of State if this program is to be used. The company must be Iowa-based and registered to qualify. Confirm the entity structure with counsel first.

6.3 Federal grants: NIH/NIDCR SBIR/STTR verify

  • NIDCR's SBIR page lists focus areas that include clinical decision support and workflow integration, and advanced dental materials and digital manufacturing. Those match parts of this product.
  • No 2026-specific NIDCR solicitation for this topic was confirmed in our search. Check the NIH Guide and the SBIR/STTR funding page before applying.
  • Important constraint: NIDCR does not accept clinical trials through its SBIR or STTR programs. Clinical validation must be funded separately (equity, partner, or other grant mechanisms).
  • A Phase I application typically needs a feasibility plan and a principal investigator eligible under SBIR rules. Engage a grant-writing advisor.

6.4 Crowdfunding: Regulation Crowdfunding

  • Regulation Crowdfunding allows an issuer to raise up to $5,000,000 in a rolling 12-month period through a registered intermediary (SEC guidance). Amounts raised in other exempt offerings within the prior 12 months do not count toward the limit.
  • Accredited investors have no investment limit. Non-accredited investors are subject to per-investor limits. One secondary source gives $124,000 per 12 months; verify against the current rule text.
  • Disclosure requirements, including financial statements and ongoing reporting, are significant. Use this route only after counsel has advised on it.
  • Recommended timing: after Stage A demonstrator results, not before. Crowdfunding for a pre-prototype medical device needs careful risk disclosure.

6.5 Research partners

Dental schools and robotics institutes can provide laboratory access, students, validated test fixtures and co-funded studies. Agree on IP ownership in writing before sharing designs.

6.6 Strategic partners

Dental equipment manufacturers, dental service organizations and dental supply companies can provide capital, distribution, manufacturing or validation resources in exchange for a license, joint development agreement or equity. Keep control of core IP.

8. Regulatory and clinical path

  • Classification: an ultrasonic dental scaler falls under 21 CFR 872.4850 as Class II. That does not determine the classification of the complete integrated machine. The combined system needs its own assessment.
  • Early engagement: submit an FDA Q-Submission before major Stage B spending, to get written feedback on intended use and pathway.
  • Quality system: the FDA Quality Management System Regulation (QMSR) took effect February 2, 2026 and incorporates ISO 13485:2016. Build the quality system from the start.
  • Standards: ISO 14971 (risk), IEC 60601 series (electrical), IEC 80601-2-60 (dental equipment), IEC 62304 (software, if applicable), ISO 7405 (biocompatibility considerations), cybersecurity guidance.
  • AI: follow the FDA Good Machine Learning Practice principles; test on diverse populations, imaging devices and conditions.
  • Infection control: follow CDC guidance on instrument reprocessing and dental-unit water quality. Ultraviolet light alone does not count as sterilization.
  • Clinical study: NIDCR SBIR cannot fund clinical trials. Budget and approvals (ethics, IRB) are needed separately.
  • Professional oversight: confirm with state dental boards and counsel who may authorize, supervise and bill for each step.

9. Intellectual property

  • Register the DentistNow Robotics business entity before sharing designs with manufacturers.
  • Commission a patentability and freedom-to-operate search. The general concept of an automated dental cleaning machine is not assumed to be patentable; specific mechanisms and methods are what to investigate.
  • Use confidentiality (NDA) and invention-assignment agreements for all contributors, including contractors and research partners.
  • Keep CAD files, source code and datasets in company-owned repositories with access logs.
  • Watch for trade-secret handling of AI training data; record provenance and consent for any patient images.

15.3 How investors get their money back

  • No cash returns before exit in the Base case. Cumulative cash before financing stays negative through Year 7, with a low point of about $9.2M in Year 6. Investors receive nothing until the company is sold, merged, or pays distributions from positive cash.
  • The main route is an exit around Year 7. In the Base case, enterprise value is about $36.7M (3x Year 7 revenue of $12.2M). Equity value including cash is about $39.8M. An investor holding 35% receives about $13.9M, or 1.32x the $10.6M raised.
  • A recurring-revenue royalty does not repay the raise. A 5% royalty on Base-case recurring revenue in Years 4–7 totals about $0.33M, against a $10.6M raise. It can be a sweetener, not the repayment route.
  • Liquidation preference. A preferred-share structure could return invested capital first in a low-value sale. It does not change the Base-case economics above, and its terms need securities counsel.

15.4 What must be true for investors to earn 3x

At a 3x Year 7 exit multiple and a 35% stake, the Base case gives 1.32x. Reaching 3x needs about 126 machines sold in Year 7, which is about 1.8 times the Base case, or a combination of higher volume, lower unit cost, and higher recurring revenue. The sensitivity table in the workbook shows the trade-off. Three levers matter most, in order: units sold, unit cost, and the exit multiple. Each needs evidence from Stage A and Stage B before this model should be used to ask for capital.

Holding the Base case fixed, each of these changes alone would give investors 3x:

15.5 Valuation check

Raising the Base-case $10.6M for 35% implies a post-money valuation of about $30.2M and a pre-money valuation of about $19.6M. That is a high valuation for a concept-stage company with no hardware. Treat the 35% stake as a placeholder. A lower valuation gives investors a larger stake and a higher multiple, but founders should expect pressure in the opposite direction. Counsel and an advisor on valuation should set this before any investor sees it.
