Quality assurance & safety

A clear standard for the work. A safe plan for the site.

Quality and safety expectations should be defined before mobilization, communicated to the team, and reviewed throughout the engagement.

Quality management approach

Quality is planned, checked, documented, and corrected.

The quality plan for each site should match the service agreement, building use, finish materials, occupancy, and client requirements. The following describes the operating framework to apply and document for each contract.

Scope verification

Confirm work areas, task frequencies, exclusions, service windows, supply responsibilities, and acceptance criteria before work begins.

Routine inspections

Use area-based inspection checklists, identify deficiencies, assign corrective action, and record completion.

Issue resolution

Log client concerns, prioritize urgent issues, assign an owner, set a target response, and confirm closure.

The final cleaning sequence and inspection criteria must be aligned with the general contractor’s specifications. Do not clean active work areas until released and safe to enter.

Site safety planning

Project-specific safety requirements take precedence. Before mobilization, confirm site orientation, hazard communication, PPE, access equipment, restricted areas, emergency procedures, and reporting requirements.

Site orientation and sign-in requirements
Hazard assessment and stop-work escalation
Required PPE and task-specific protection
Chemical labeling and Safety Data Sheets
Slip, trip, fall, and wet-floor controls
Ladder / lift / elevated-work authorization
Electrical, mechanical, and restricted-area awareness
Waste handling and disposal instructions
Incident, near-miss, and property-damage reporting
Emergency contacts and evacuation procedures

This page describes a framework, not a representation that a particular certification, written program, training record, or insurance endorsement is currently held. Those documents must be verified.
